# FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS RULES
# drdip-report-writer v1.0
# Development Response to Displacement Impacts Project II (DRDIP-II)
# Use this guide when writing or reviewing the findings and recommendations sections of any DRDIP-II report.

---

## PART A — DEFINITIONS

### What is a finding?

A finding is a factual statement about what was observed, measured, or verified during a mission, training, supervision, or monitoring activity.

- A finding describes what EXISTS or what WAS OBSERVED.
- A finding is attributed to a specific location and time.
- A finding does not contain a recommendation.

**Examples of correct findings:**
- "The water supply system in Dolo-Ado Kebele X was 75% complete as of May 2026."
- "Twelve of the 20 women's savings groups in Bokolmayo Woreda had active monthly meetings during Q2."
- "Rangeland enclosures in Danot Woreda showed visible vegetation recovery compared to the previous year."

**Examples of incorrect findings (these are actually recommendations, not findings):**
- "The project should accelerate construction of the water supply system." — this is a recommendation
- "Women's savings groups need more support." — this is a recommendation
- "Rangeland management should be extended to more kebeles." — this is a recommendation

---

### What is a challenge?

A challenge is an obstacle that is preventing or slowing implementation. Challenges belong in Section 9.

- A challenge describes WHY something is not working as planned.
- A challenge is not the same as a finding. A finding describes what was observed. A challenge explains an obstacle.
- Do not repeat Section 8 findings in Section 9.

**Examples of correct challenges:**
- "Delayed release of quarterly funds from the regional bureau affected procurement timelines in three woredas."
- "Shortage of trained animal health workers in Bookh Woreda limited the reach of livestock productivity activities."

**Examples of incorrect challenges (these belong in Section 8 as findings, not Section 9):**
- "The water system is only 75% complete." — this is a finding
- "Only 12 of 20 savings groups are active." — this is a finding

---

### What is a recommendation?

A recommendation is a specific action that someone should take to address a finding or challenge.

- A recommendation describes what SHOULD BE DONE.
- A recommendation names a responsible party.
- A recommendation references the specific finding or challenge it addresses.
- Every recommendation must have a corresponding action matrix entry.

---

## PART B — RULES FOR WRITING FINDINGS

1. State facts only. Do not state opinions, judgements, or conclusions that go beyond what was directly observed.
2. Attribute every finding to the specific location where it was observed (woreda and kebele where possible).
3. Use numbers when they are available. "Three of five subprojects" is better than "some subprojects."
4. Separate positive findings from areas requiring attention. Do not mix them in the same list.
5. Do not combine a finding with a recommendation in one sentence.
6. Do not use hedging phrases in findings: "it appears that," "it seems," "reportedly."
7. Do not write findings about things you did not directly observe or verify. Flag them as unverified: `[CLARIFY: this point could not be directly verified during the mission — please confirm]`

---

## PART C — PROHIBITED PHRASES IN FINDINGS

Do not use these phrases in Section 8:

| Prohibited Phrase | Why It Is Wrong | Replace With |
|---|---|---|
| "it is recommended that..." | Belongs in Section 10, not Section 8 | State the finding only; put the recommendation in Section 10 |
| "the project should..." | This is a recommendation | State what was observed; save the "should" for Section 10 |
| "it appears that..." | Hedging — state what was actually observed | State the finding directly or flag it as unverified |
| "reportedly..." | Unverified — use only if you explicitly flag it | State the source and flag as unverified |
| "it is hoped that..." | Not a finding | Remove; this is not observable |
| "the community expressed that..." | A quote without attribution | Attribute to a specific person or group; put in Community Feedback section |

---

## PART D — RULES FOR WRITING RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Begin every recommendation with an action verb from the approved list below.
2. Name the responsible party. "The project" is not a responsible party. Name the specific unit, officer, or organisation.
3. Reference the finding or challenge the recommendation addresses. The reader must be able to see the connection.
4. Be specific. State exactly what should be done, not just that improvement is needed.
5. Every recommendation must have a corresponding entry in the Action Matrix.
6. Do not duplicate recommendations from a previous report unless the action was not completed.

---

## PART E — APPROVED ACTION VERB LIST

Use one of these verbs to begin every recommendation:

| Verb | When to Use |
|---|---|
| Allocate | When additional resources (budget, staff, materials) are needed |
| Assign | When a specific person or unit needs to be made responsible for a task |
| Conduct | When a specific activity (training, meeting, assessment) needs to happen |
| Develop | When a new document, plan, system, or tool needs to be created |
| Ensure | When an existing requirement is not being met and needs to be enforced |
| Establish | When a new structure, group, mechanism, or procedure needs to be created |
| Monitor | When regular oversight or tracking of something needs to be formalised |
| Provide | When materials, inputs, training, or technical support need to be supplied |
| Review | When an existing plan, document, or practice needs to be assessed and revised |
| Strengthen | When an existing system or capacity is insufficient and needs to be improved |

## PART F — BEFORE AND AFTER EXAMPLES

### Example 1 — Vague vs specific recommendation

**Vague (incorrect):**
"The project should improve the safeguards compliance situation in Kebribeyah."

**Specific (correct):**
"Assign the Environmental and Social Safeguards Officer to conduct a site visit to all active subprojects in Kebribeyah Woreda within 30 days to assess and document environmental mitigation measures, in response to the partial compliance finding noted in Section 8.2."

---

### Example 2 — Recommendation misplaced as a finding

**Wrong (in Section 8 — Findings):**
"The project should ensure that rangeland committees are formed before the next rainy season."

**Correct — as a finding (in Section 8):**
"Rangeland committees had not been formed in four of the eight target kebeles in Danot Woreda as of the mission date."

**Correct — as a recommendation (in Section 10):**
"Ensure that rangeland committee formation is completed in the remaining four kebeles in Danot Woreda before [target date], with the Community Development Officer assigned as the responsible party."

---

### Example 3 — No responsible party

**Wrong:**
"Training on financial management should be conducted for woreda-level staff."

**Correct:**
"Conduct a two-day financial management refresher training for woreda project coordinators in all eight target woredas, to be organised by the DRDIP-II Regional Project Coordination Unit by [target date], in response to the financial reporting gaps identified in Section 9."

---

## PART G — LINKING RECOMMENDATIONS TO THE ACTION MATRIX

Every recommendation in Section 10 must appear in the Action Matrix (Section 11).

The action matrix entry must include:
- The action item (a brief version of the recommendation — one sentence)
- The responsible party named in the recommendation
- A specific target date (DD/MM/YYYY) — not "end of quarter" or "as soon as possible"
- Status: Pending (for new recommendations)
- Remarks: any context needed for follow-up

If the target date is not known, insert: `[MISSING: target date]`
Do not write "TBD" or leave the field blank.
